The viewer lights the model with the real sun: SunCalc for its position, the building's approximate orientation from OpenStreetMap, and Rome time. Scrubbing through a day shows when the sun faces the street windows and when it moves behind the frontage.

{{< clip src="/visuals/research/ambient-computing/2026-10-04-daylight.mp4" poster="/visuals/research/ambient-computing/2026-10-04-daylight.jpg" alt="The studio model in cutaway as the time slider moves from morning to evening and the room lights switch on" caption="A day from 07:30 to 19:30, with the room lights switched on at dusk." >}}

That matters beyond looks. The frontage faces east, which puts cooling the studio early on hot mornings on the list of [first automations](/research/ambient-computing/first-automations/). Nearby buildings and trees aren't in the model yet.
