Hello Walkers—

Ludo and I arrived in Paris at noon on Sunday, November 8. The flight from Rome was short. I love Europe for that. After twelve years of reaching Europe by way of [a narrow chair in the sky][chair], it felt like cheating.

We were there for Paris Photo week with one handbound book, a stack of postcards, and a plan that required me to talk to strangers.

On Monday, I dropped off the book at [FotoFilmic Paris][show] in the Marais and decided to walk home. Home for the week was in Saint-Germain-des-Prés, on the other side of the river. It had been thirteen or fourteen years since I'd last been to Paris, and I remembered a much grubbier city. Tokyo has made my standards for a clean street unreasonable, so take that as a compliment. I like it in November. All those earthy colors go very well with a gray sky.

![A bridge across the Seine with the Eiffel Tower in the distance.](/flaneur/018_seine.jpeg)

The book is [*Scent of Time*][scent], the one I finished binding back in July. Eight of us from [Greg Girard's][greg] group at FotoFilmic had books in the show. For Paris Photo week, you could come in off the street and leaf through the lot.

Before the opening on Tuesday, Ludo and I went to the Musée d'Orsay. There's a winter painting by Monet I particularly wanted to see: [*The Magpie*][magpie], a field of snow with one small black bird sitting on a gate. The snow looks whiter than I thought you could get out of white paint, and the shadows are blue the way they come out in photographs, which isn't how my eyes usually report them. Looking at it does something funny to my brain, a bit like looking at one of James Turrell's light installations, except here it's all paint.

![Toto looking at Monet's The Magpie at the Musée d'Orsay.](/flaneur/018_monet.jpeg)

Then the opening. A lot of nice people showed up. In [the last dispatch][last], I said I was looking forward to standing nearby, pretending not to watch, when someone picked the book up. People picked it up. I stood nearby. The pretending needs work.

In October, I wrote to 83 publishers about the book. I can do that sort of thing from my desk. A few replied: some were kindly full for the next two or three years, and some said to come by and say hello.

So I had postcards printed with a link to the maquette. At the Grand Palais on Wednesday, all I had to do was walk up to a publisher, say hello, and hand one over. For an introvert like me, that's the most difficult part of making a book.

I have no confidence for that sort of thing. Ludo has plenty. She walked up to stand after stand and got us talking, and between us we gave cards to about a dozen publishers, I think. She was a trooper, and I'm extremely grateful to her.

![A hand holds a Scent of Time postcard above the exhibition stands under the glass roof of the Grand Palais.](/flaneur/018_postcard.jpeg)

The vernissage was overwhelming. The number of galleries and publishers under that glass roof is staggering, and we were there for almost eight hours. We were about to leave when a message came in: an assistant at one of the publishers had passed my postcard to her boss, and could I come by? He spent about half an hour with me, going through the book. I won't say more until there's more to say. Fingers crossed.

So at least one of the postcards has earned its keep.

---

On Thursday, we took a break from photography to go see what Tadao Ando had done with the Bourse de Commerce: a concrete cylinder set inside the old round exchange hall, impressive and bold. There was a photography (duh) exhibition on. We saw that too.

Then we went over to [Polycopies][polycopies], the photobook fair on a boat moored in front of the Orsay, which is a dangerous place for anyone who likes buying photobooks. I bought two. Given a bigger baggage allowance and a slightly thicker wallet, I think I'd have bought at least two dozen.

On Friday, we were back at the Grand Palais for the 11 am opening. If you want to talk to the publishers at Paris Photo, you've got to go early. I talked to a few more of them, and managed some of the hellos myself this time. Around lunchtime, the place started to resemble Shinjuku Station, so we went down to the main floor to look at the galleries.

I keep thinking about how different those two ends of the same fair are. It's all photography, but the photographers on the gallery walls downstairs are so different from the ones in the books upstairs. I understand why. I still felt much more at home with the people making books.

After lunch, we went to l'Orangerie to see Monet's massive Water Lilies. Absolutely breathtaking. That's two Monets in a week that was supposed to be about photography.

On Saturday, Ludo took me up Montmartre, and we walked back across the city in time for the closing at FotoFilmic.

---

By Sunday, our smart devices reckoned we'd walked about 70 km in seven and a half days. (For a newsletter called The Flâneur, it was about time.) We slept nine hours, went out for brunch, and by the time I sat down to write in my journal, Ludo was asleep again on the sofa behind me.

She'd earned it.

A few conversations about the book are still going. I'll tell you when there's something to tell.

More soon,

—Toto
