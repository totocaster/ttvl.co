## Gear, Gear, Gear

Recently, a few friends discovered my Mercari page, leading to jokes about how I've seemingly owned every piece of photographic gear ever produced. From the outside, this revolving door of equipment might suggest endless indecision or obsession. But there was a method to this madness.

There's a common phrase in photography circles -- "Gear doesn't matter." usually it's is been evoked by poeple who want to make point that it actually doesn't matter. While I agree with sentiment of "better camera euqls better photographs," I do think gear, as any other tool, does matter; but not ina  way most poeple think.

Tools, in my world, address human needs by amplifying human capabilities. They fit into two interfaces -- need and human. First part of the tool fits _the problem_ and seconds fits _the operator_. 

Chisel has a sharp end to dulfill a need, and a handle, to tif a human. Camera has a lens and a sensor to make an image and hads pretty much all of the rest -- handl, shutter button, screen and menus (or not), viewfinder, stap-lugs and many more things -- to fit the operator. If you spend even tiniest of time in YouTube or forum photosphere, you'll quickly reslise how much poeple talk about the image-making part of the tool rather than latter. 

I'm glad those YouTube channels and forums exists—it's qyute easy to undertand camera capabilities becaue of them online. Hoever what you can never learn from those resoucres is how it fits you—the operator.

Yearly in 2024 I decided to my problem with a brutforce approach of biuying every camera I ever dreamed of in sequence and try them out in a field. Fortunatly being in Japan helps as used camera market is vekry healthy and you can resell the camera for the same or little loss.

I did exacly that and found some intersting surpises not only regarding camras, but also my composition preferences. I was _"sure"_ sqare format is "not for me" and "it's hard to compose." (That's what they they on forums). But I found 6x6 format to be extreamly pleaassant constaint to work with. 

I also discovered most of the "best" cameras feel incredably uncomfortable to me. And that's great, I have one less camera system to buy/dream/want on my list.

I also managed to buy a pareticularly 35mm camera _twice_ just to come to a pice that I do not enjoy shooting my color work on _any_ 35mm camera -- so all of that got schatched form a list.

Legenrady Leica for a street -- turns out I shoot a lot with one hand, which makes all Leica scrached for a "dream camera" list -- in fact all manual focus cameras. 

This leads me to a culmination of the expetiment.

## The Gear-Freeze

The Gear Freeze isn't about settling or resigning oneself to limitations. Instead, it represents clarity—_definitive choice_. After thorough exploration, after questioning assumptions and challenging biases, you commit fully to a single setup. It's a self-imposed contract stating clearly: "This is it., i found it!" The exploration period was essential, not a distraction. It was a deliberate, guilt-free search for the tool that feels nice and fits a workflow.

## Freeze

Once decided, the Gear Freeze demands full focus. Unsubscribe from gear channels, avoid forums, and ignore new releases (What is the point watching or reading all of those? You already have everything you need, right?). Eliminate distraction. This shift—from exploring tools to mastering your craft—is challenging but necessary. By stepping away from the endless loop of gear evaluation and stepping fully into creation, the real work begins. The Gear Freeze isn't about limiting possibilities; it’s about finally unlocking them.
