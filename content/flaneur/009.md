## The Past Year: Travels and a New Darkroom

This year, 2024, was hectic. I traveled on 24 long-haul flights across the globe, bouncing between Europe, Japan, and all points in between. It was also a pivotal time in my photography practice. I’ve completed my shift to analog processes, including building out a proper darkroom in my new studio with my girlfriend. It’s been a long journey getting a dedicated space where I can develop both black-and-white and color prints. I’m excited to share more about what I’ve learned along the way—everything from approaching photographic projects and bookmaking to refining my gear and workflow.

## ttvl.co: A Public Notebook

I’ve launched a new website, [ttvl.co](https://ttvl.co), where I plan to share all my current work—photography-related or otherwise. Think of it as a public notebook of my findings, ideas, and curiosities. It already hosts a few sections:

- **The Log**: A distilled version of my daily journal (minus personal stuff).
- **Projects and Tools**: Smaller experiments and utilities I’ve built, mainly geared toward photography.
- **Workflows**: Documentation on how I organize everything. Efficiency is key to producing quality work.
- **Darkroom Section**: Technical articles about analog processes and photography at large.

I’ve also been pursuing research in human-computer interaction, and I’m starting to share some of those findings too. The first chapter is about my notebook system, “Unbound,” which merges the benefits of pen-and-paper with the search and retrieval powers of digital tools. It’s a work in progress, but I hope others might find it useful or want to contribute.

## Moving Beyond Substack

One more thing: this newsletter has also migrated to the new site. I like that it gives me control over my own infrastructure. Yes, it costs me a bit, but I trust the platform more than a giant tech corporation.

## New Websites and AI-Assisted Coding

I’ve been coding a few websites lately—not just [ttvl.co](https://ttvl.co) but others I’ll reveal in the coming months. It’s been a while since I last did heavy web development, but this time I had the advantage of using AI-assisted coding tools. It turns out programming may not be as “safe” from automation as people once assumed. The AI handles much of the technical heavy-lifting, and my role is more like a senior developer, guiding and reviewing. With its help, I managed to launch five complete websites in about a month.

I’m looking forward to 2025; it promises to be an exciting year full of new projects and discoveries. Stay tuned for more updates, announcements, and ideas.

Feel free to explore [ttvl.co](https://ttvl.co) and let me know what you think. I hope you’ll find something there that sparks your curiosity or helps your own work in some way.
