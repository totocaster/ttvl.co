Hello fellow walkers and flâneurs,

It’s been a while since we last connected. I'm Toto and you are reading The Flaneur — a long‑neglected newsletter of mine that has been on a stable—an annual schedule for a couple of years now.

If you are new, welcome—and thank you for remembering me (fingers crossed).

If not, you might notice the newsletter looks a bit different than usual. That's because of the big changes I’ll share right after these paragraphs.

## The Past Year: Travels and a New Darkroom

2024 was hectic. I traveled on 24 long-haul flights across the globe, bouncing between Europe, Japan, and all points in between. It was also a pivotal time in my photography practice. I’ve completed my shift to analog processes, including building out a proper darkroom in my new studio. It’s been a long journey getting a dedicated space where I can develop both black-and-white and color prints and film. I’m excited to share more about what I’ve learned along the way—everything from approaching photographic projects and bookmaking to refining my gear and workflow.

The log is a distilled version of my daily journal (minus personal things).

## Moving Beyond Substack

One more thing: this newsletter has also migrated to the new site. I like that it gives me control over my own infrastructure. Yes, it costs me a bit, but I trust the platform more than a giant tech corporation.

## Programming and AI-Assisted Coding

I’ve been coding a few websites lately—not just [ttvl.co](https://ttvl.co) but others I’ll reveal in the coming months. (Spoilers—one of them is an updated photography-only website, replacing the current [totocaster.com](https://totocaster.com))

It’s been a while since I last did heavy web development, but this time I had the advantage of using AI-assisted coding tools. I find this collaboration quite interesting and eye-opening. The AI handles much of the technical heavy-lifting, and my role is more like a senior developer, guiding and reviewing. With its help, I managed to write four complete websites in about a month.

I find it a quite interesting experience. I clearly remember in the early GPT days, pundits were counting down the years—even months—for creative workers while claiming that software engineers would be safe forever. If anything, this experience has clearly demonstrated to me that AI acts as an extremely technically proficient developer with almost no creativity at all.
