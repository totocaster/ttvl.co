Jinny Street Gallery is a contemporary art space uniquely created from streetlamps in the walkable and charming neighborhood of Jingumae 2-chome, Shibuya, Tokyo. Just north of Harajuku.

I founded this project with my friend [Lorenzo Menghi][lorenzo] in January 2023. The gallery’s mission is to showcase emerging artists across a wide range of styles and mediums while promoting [walking](/notes/walking/).

As my dear friend [Rafael Balboa][rafa] once described it, Jinny Street Gallery is an inverted conventional gallery where the indoor space has transformed into the outdoors, right on the street.

Jinny Street Gallery consists of 42 streetlamps with integrated display cases, scattered throughout the Jingumae 2-chome neighborhood ([see map][jsgmap]). To experience the gallery, you’ll need to walk it.

The exhibitions are free to visit, day or night. Alongside them, we organize artist walks, workshops, and collaborations with people and businesses in the neighborhood. The gallery has become a place to meet people and make things together.

Before Jinny opened, I tried the display cases with _Walk With Me_ in spring 2022: 59 of my street photographs across all 42 lamps. Our first exhibition as Jinny was _Tokyo Flâneur_, which opened on January 15, 2023. The [exhibition archive](https://www.jinnystreetgallery.com/exhibitions/) follows the shows since then, from photography and painting to sculpture and installations made for the cases.

## Artist Walks

On our artist walks, we go through the exhibition together, stopping at the lamps to hear the artists talk about their work. A show becomes a route through the neighborhood, with conversations along the way. Many of the walks are part of an opening night, gathering at nearby NININI·NI before heading out into the street.

## Workshops and the Creators’ Club

The [Creators’ Club](https://www.jinnystreetgallery.com/exhibitions/thecreatorsclub/) began in October 2024 with Altria of Owlpaca Studio. Its workshops take ideas from Jinny’s exhibitions and turn them into something people can try themselves, using everyday materials. No art training is needed.

We have made collages, painted over photographs, drawn one another, and built sculptures from discarded bottles. Other sessions have taken us outside to collect rubbings of the city’s surfaces, or brought everyone together to paint tiles and make small books. A workshop with Kittyzilla invited people to draw their dream neighborhood in a folded zine; another produced sandwich-shaped “Sando Zines.”

The work came back into the streetlamps in the [Creators’ Club exhibition](https://www.jinnystreetgallery.com/exhibitions/thecreatorsclub/), from December 2025 to January 2026. People who had joined us to try something for an afternoon became exhibitors themselves.

## Made with the Neighborhood

We work with the Jingumae 2-chome Shopping Association, its director Sawabe Kin, and the people who live and run businesses here. Openings at NININI·NI and collaborations with nearby places are part of the gallery’s life.

Open calls bring new people into the program. [_Lemon Roads_](https://www.jinnystreetgallery.com/exhibitions/lemon-roads/) brought together 42 artists around the launch of Jinny Lemon Sour. For [_Yu-Asobi_](https://www.jinnystreetgallery.com/exhibitions/yu-asobi/), we worked with Kosugiyu Harajuku on an exhibition about public bathing, including artists chosen through an open call to the bathhouse’s visitors.

[_Jinny Playground_](https://www.jinnystreetgallery.com/exhibitions/jinnyplayground/) extended this into play: interactive works in and around the lamps, with a treasure hunt through the neighborhood. The street itself became part of the exhibition.

## Jinny Lemon Sour

Jinny Lemon Sour is no longer made. We stopped producing it in January 2026.

- Website: [jinnystreetgallery.com/lemon-sour](https://www.jinnystreetgallery.com/lemon-sour)
- Instagram: [@jinny.sour](https://www.instagram.com/jinny.sour)

Jinny Lemon Sour was a collaboration between Jinny Street Gallery and [Open Book][ob], a renowned bar and beverage-maker in Shinjuku’s legendary Golden Gai district. Open Book made the craft lemon sours, while Jinny Street Gallery curated artworks from our featured artists for the drink’s labels.

It was sold only in the gallery’s neighborhood; the [map of the shops that carried it][jlsmap] stays here for the record.
