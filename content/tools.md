{{< toc title="On this page" >}}

Every tool has two interfaces. The first meets the problem; the second meets the operator. A camera has a lens and a sensor to make an image, and everything else, the grip, the shutter button, the viewfinder, exists to fit the photographer. Most of what follows stayed because of the second.

Choosing is half of it. Once a tool feels right, I stop looking: no gear channels, no forums, no release news. I call it a [gear-freeze](/flaneur/010/), a self-imposed contract that says, "This is it. I've found it. Now, make." The second one began in 2024, with the darkroom. Everything is [drawn](/figures/) to scale, at the makers' published sizes.

{{< section title="Walk" id="walk" >}}

[Walking](/notes/walking/) is where a lot of this begins. I do much of my thinking on [long walks](/traces/walks/), usually with a camera in hand. I don't wear headphones. Twenty kilometers is a normal way to think.

What comes along is small. A Teenage Engineering TP-7 for voice notes, and [a CLI I wrote](/project-humane/tp7/) to get them off it. An [Unbound notebook](/project-humane/notebook-system/). An iPhone 17 Pro, in orange. A Casio G-Shock on one wrist and a Whoop on the other, read by [Whoopy](/project-humane/whoopy/).

{{< drawing src="/visuals/tools/2026-11-02-walk-on-me.svg" width="wide" alt="Front views to scale, standing on one line: the Teenage Engineering TP-7, an Unbound notebook with its six rings, the back of an iPhone 17 Pro, a Casio G-Shock, and a Whoop band." >}}

The bags are Freitag, sewn from old truck tarps: an F155 Clapton for everything and an F05 Blair for money. For travel, several F35 Cheyenne carry stationery, toiletries, and electronics.

{{< section title="Shoot" id="shoot" >}}

The rule is two bodies with two lenses over one body with seven: a lens stays mounted, and the pairing is the unit. The Leica M11-P is the primary body, with the close-focus Summilux-M 50mm f/1.4 ASPH. The Leica Q3 43 is the fixed-lens pairing.

{{< drawing src="/visuals/tools/2026-11-02-shoot-cameras.svg" width="wide" alt="Front views to scale: the Leica M11-P with its 50mm Summilux, the Leica Q3 43, the Contax G2 with the Biogon 28mm, the Ricoh GR IV Monochrome, the Konica Big Mini F, and cartridges of HP5 Plus, Portra, and Ultramax." >}}

Since 2017, a camera has come along on every walk, and the photographs became [_Out of Memory_](https://toto.photo/out-of-memory). For the volumes it's a Contax G2, mostly with the G 28mm f/2.8 or the G 35mm f/2, and the Ricoh GR IV Monochrome, my fourth GR.

Two Konica Big Mini F take the family rolls, 80–100 a year. HP5 Plus for black and white. Portra or Ultramax for color. The M11-P's other lens is the Summilux-M 35mm f/1.4 ASPH FLE.

Rolls get [canister stickers](/darkroom/canister-stickers/) and a [roll ID](/darkroom/roll-id/), cameras a [foldable reminder](/darkroom/foldable-film-reminder/) of what's loaded, and the rest waits in [rugged 40-roll storage](/darkroom/rugged-40-roll-film-storage/).

{{< drawing src="/visuals/tools/2026-11-02-shoot-lenses.svg" width="wide" alt="Side views to scale, each lens standing on its mount: the Summilux-M 50mm and 35mm, then all seven Contax G lenses, from the flat Hologon 16mm to the Sonnar 90mm and the Vario-Sonnar 35–70mm, with the 28mm and 35mm marked as mostly used." >}}

I own all of the G lenses, because it makes me feel good.

Inside the studio, the original Panasonic Lumix S5 is the utility camera, with the Lumix S 20–60mm, the 100mm macro, and the 50mm f/1.8. It also scans.

{{< drawing src="/visuals/tools/2026-11-02-shoot-studio.svg" width="wide" alt="To scale: the Panasonic Lumix S5 from the front with the 20–60mm mounted, and side views of the Lumix S 20–60mm, 100mm macro, and 50mm f/1.8." >}}

{{< section title="Develop" id="develop" >}}

In 2024, I built a darkroom and bookmaking studio in Kyojima, Tokyo. It was the production space until August 2026, when four hundred kilograms of it left for Italy in seventeen parcels. It now survives on this site as [_Kyojima Studio, 2024–2026_](/traces/kyojima-studio/).

{{< drawing src="/visuals/tools/2026-11-02-develop-darkroom.svg" width="wide" alt="Front views to scale: the JOBO CPE-3 processor with the Lift and a 1500 tank on its rollers, and the four-probe thermometer panel, about 250 mm wide." >}}

The processor is a JOBO CPE-3 with the Lift. Its own temperature monitoring isn't as accurate as color development needs, so I built a [panel for four probes](/darkroom/thermometer-panel-for-jobo-systems/): both sides of the tank, the developer, and inside the drum. Replenished C-41 mixed from minilab concentrates. RA-4 color prints come out of a [Lucky CP32](/darkroom/lucky-cp32-manual/).

Away from home, a [powder-based kit](/darkroom/travel-dev-kit/) lets me shoot, develop, scan, and publish on the same day.

{{< /section >}}

{{< section title="Scan" id="scan" >}}

Scanning is a camera on a copy stand. The Lumix S5 hangs from an LPL DS-4 on a Leica Bellows-R, with Apo-Rodagon optics, over a Valoi easy120 carrier.

{{< drawing src="/visuals/tools/2026-11-02-scan-copy-stand.svg" width="wide" alt="Side view of the scanning rig to scale: the LPL DS-4 copy stand holding a Lumix S5 on a Leica Bellows-R with an Apo-Rodagon, over the Valoi easy120 carrier on the VNS adapter and a Negative Supply light." >}}

Under the carrier is the [VNS adapter](/darkroom/valoi-easy120-with-negative-supply-99cri-light/), which sets the Valoi holder on a 99 CRI light from Negative Supply. With it, the color casts in the shadows went away. Negative Lab Pro for conversions. Two minutes a roll.

A Valoi easy35 for travel, black and white only.

{{< section title="Edit" id="edit" >}}

The range-topping 14-inch MacBook Pro, with the M4 Max, and an Apple Studio Display. Next to the display, bound notebooks of my own, still in development. A Stream Deck + XL runs the studio, [ambient computing](/research/ambient-computing/), and [Refmode](/project-humane/refmode/), which switches the display's reference modes without opening System Settings.

{{< drawing src="/visuals/tools/2026-11-02-edit-desk.svg" width="wide" alt="Front views to scale: the Apple Studio Display, a bound notebook, the 14-inch MacBook Pro, the Mac mini, and the Stream Deck + XL with its 36 keys and six dials." >}}

The Mac mini never sleeps. It runs the home server, and [Hank](/research/hank/) lives there.

Capture One for photographs. [Obsidian](/obsidian/), with [Arrowhead](/obsidian/arrowhead/) underneath it. Things for tasks, through [Things CLI](/project-humane/things-cli/). Day One for the daily digital journal I've kept since 2019. Claude Code and Codex for code, used [this way](/ai/). Hugo for this site, as the [Colophon](/colophon/) explains.

{{< section title="Make" id="make" >}}

A3 kraft paper for ideation, and Pilot FriXion Waai pens, 0.5 mm, in black, gray, and orange. Notes go into the [Unbound Notebook System](/project-humane/notebook-system/): loose leaves in covers I make myself.

{{< drawing src="/visuals/tools/2026-11-02-make-pens.svg" width="wide" alt="To scale: three Pilot FriXion Waai pens with black, gray, and orange ink bands, and a bible-size Unbound leaf with six holes." >}}

InDesign for books. Fusion 360 for parts, most of them in [Darkroom](/darkroom/). I've printed [_Out of Memory_](https://toto.photo/out-of-memory) in 200-page volumes since 2019, and the other books I make [by hand](/bookbinding/).

{{< section title="Retired" id="retired" >}}

The first [gear-freeze](/flaneur/010/) was a Leica M-D with a 50mm Summilux: six years and tens of thousands of frames. Earlier volumes of _Out of Memory_ were also shot on a Sony RX100 VII, a Leica Q2 Monochrom, and many Ricoh GR IIIs.

Film cameras I used and let go: a Konica Hexar RF, a Fujifilm Klasse S, a Mamiya RZ67 Pro II, a Hasselblad 203FE, a Shen Hao 6×17, and a Horseman 45FA. Two left something here: a [fine-focus gear](/darkroom/rz67-focus-gear-stl/) for the RZ67 and [lensboards](/darkroom/horseman-lensboards/) for the Horseman.
