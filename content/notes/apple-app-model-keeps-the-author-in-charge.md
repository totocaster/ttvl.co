title: "Apple’s app model keeps the author in charge"
date: 2026-10-19T00:00:00+09:00
category: thinking
draft: true
description: "Personal software should change with its user, not remain bounded by its author’s decisions."
---

Apple’s app model assumes somebody makes a tool, gets it reviewed, and distributes it. I can change what I put into it, but what the tool does remains the author’s decision.

[Software gets malleable when describing it is enough](/notes/software-gets-malleable/). Today a small tool takes an afternoon. If models become fast and reliable enough, a request could produce its own interface: not just the information, but the controls for exploring it or acting on it. Both the content and the medium become changeable.

Small utilities might not survive as products. Why find an application that almost fits when I can describe the behavior I need? It might not become an application at all. Just something available when I ask.

Maintenance matters, but fixing bugs is only part of it. My life changes. Keeping a tool fitted to it is maintenance too. An application can be actively maintained while preserving assumptions that no longer work for me.

The author’s taste can be a good starting point. It should not have to remain the limit. Unless Apple makes room for software its users can reshape, its model of personal computing will feel increasingly impersonal.
