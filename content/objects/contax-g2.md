The Contax G2 wasn’t an obvious choice for me. Its electronic features initially felt intimidating, especially after years of scaremongering from YouTube photography community of anything electronic in film cameras. (William Sheepskin’s [4-year experience][sheepskin] video is the one exception I’d recommend for anyone curious about the G2.) But after years with the purely mechanical Leica M2 and a positive experience with the Fuji Klasse, I was ready to explore what the G2 had to offer.

The Contax G system excels in several ways, but its lenses are its greatest strength. The Biogon 21mm and 28mm lenses are phenomenal, and the system is as easy to use as a point-and-shoot. These wide-angle lenses convinced me to fully commit to the G2, though I’ve since acquired the complete lens set.

The G2’s flash TTL metering is another standout feature, especially for fill-flash. The camera’s metering is so precise that I rarely use manual shutter speed selection. It also offers a maximum AUTO shutter speed of 1/6000—higher than the 1/4000 most sources claim—which means I can often skip ND filter in my [EDC carry][edc].

I own two G2 bodies, nicknamed _Daido_ and _Araki_ for easier logging in my film journal. If you’re considering this system, I recommend the champagne-finished models. They’re significantly more affordable than the black versions, which are priced exorbitantly and offer no practical advantages. In Japan, black models can cost twice as much, and accessories for them—like the GD-2 databack—are rare and overpriced, sometimes fetching triple their usual price.

Speaking of the GD-2 Databack, it’s one of my favorite accessories. It can imprint the date and time of each photo _between_ the frames and even summarize metadata from the entire roll on the first two frames. This ingenious feature allows to log details without additional tools. For projects like _Out of Memory_, it’s been a game-changer. There’s no need to disrupt the flow of shooting by pulling out a notebook to jot down timestamps manually.
