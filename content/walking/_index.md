Walking is a cure. I discovered this in my late teens. Whenever I felt sad, I'd go out and start walking without a destination or sense of time. I walked until it felt right. This became my secret weapon for my mental health. Within a couple of years, this prescription became a habit. I walked even when I felt good. It helped with my depression and enabled me to explore many of my favorite places.

## Meditation

I have tried several forms of meditation. To me, long walks is a form of meditation. The rhythm of footsteps, the steady pace, the immersion in surroundings — they create a mental space where thoughts can settle and untangle.

I do not wear heaphones on long walks. Everything can be distraction. Having camera in a hand sharpens the eye; photos might not come up great, but the mind is grateful for the experiance of having three to five hours of complete piece and tranquility.

## "Operating System"

I stole this term from one of [Craig Mod][cm]'s numerous wonderful newsletters. I like the phrase and I like the idea behind it -- using a walk as an operating system for creative production, a means of inspiration for producing a tangable output.

## Street Photography

I was taking photos casually from a very early age, but I never connected it with walking, as apparent as it seems. In 2017, I decided to carry a camera whenever I went out and started to document my walks.

In some odd way, this has become a documentation of the state of my mind rather than the streets. I took photos of everything my brain would fixate on. My hands obediently followed the stream of thoughts in my head. I pushed the shutter release without any prejudice or analysis.

As a photographer, this was new for me. New and liberating.

## _Out of Memory_

The _Out of Memory_ series emerged from my habit of carrying a camera everywhere I go and shooting without any specific purpose—documenting myself moving through time and life. Coincidentally, I also started keeping a written daily journal. Over time, _Out of Memory_ became an illustrated version of that journal.

Every six to eight months, a memory card in my camera would fill up (hence the name). I’d dump the images onto my hard drive, cull them, sequence them, and create a new volume—a 200-or-so-page book.

Today, the memory card has been replaced by a single sheet of my [PHNS](/notebook-system/) page. On this sheet, I log 36–40 rolls of film. Once the page is full, I develop the rolls, and the cycle starts all over again.

Many of the photos you see in my {{< membership-link >}}membership program{{< /membership-link >}} zine series, _I Dream With One Eye Open_, originate from this ongoing project.
