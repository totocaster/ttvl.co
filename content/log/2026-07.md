- Launched [*Traces*](/traces/), a new series of scans, photographs, documents, and other records of my life and work, beginning with [*Kyojima Studio, 2024–2026*](/traces/kyojima-studio/)—an interactive 3D scan preserving the studio just before packing began.
- Added [*A Spatial Long Exposure*](/traces/spatial-long-exposure/) to the series, collapsing 38,403 points from my location history and geotagged photographs into a single undated map.
- Finished a second, updated hand-bound maquette of [*Scent of Time*](/flaneur/014/) for its Paris remount during Paris Photo week, incorporating the program feedback through another sequencing pass, color adjustments, printing, and several days at the bindery.
- Published [Patchcord](/project-humane/patchcord/) and its [source code](https://github.com/totocaster/patchcord), an agent-friendly workflow for building and debugging CircuitPython projects, then tested it successfully with a Hello World build on an ESP32 board.
- Finished packing the Kyojima studio’s books, tools, and equipment for the move to Italy: 17 surface-mail parcels weighing more than 400 kilograms. Apparently a creative practice is also a freight category.
- Secured an Italian Type D long-stay visa, one of the less photogenic but fairly essential parts of relocating.
- Spent three days at [rural 2026](https://ruraljp.com/en/); [Jane Fitz](https://ruraljp.com/en/projects/jane-fitz/)’s five-hour all-vinyl set was the musical highlight.
- Revisited [*HIROSHI SUGIMOTO: EXTINCTION*](https://www.momat.go.jp/en/exhibitions/hiroshi-sugimoto-extinction) at the National Museum of Modern Art, Tokyo.
- Escaped central Tokyo twice for riverside breaks near Musashi-Itsukaichi and in Urasa, Niigata.
- Started rewatching [*The Lord of the Rings*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_(film_series))—extended editions, naturally—and revisited [*Dune: Part Two*](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt15239678/), which held up better on a second viewing.
- Watched [*Exit Through the Gift Shop*](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt1587707/) for the first time. It was amazing; I am surprised I had somehow missed it until now.

July was a month of preserving things at the exact moment I was taking them apart. I made a spatial record of the Kyojima studio, finished a new physical version of a book built from years of photographs, and then packed the studio’s working life into boxes. There is something almost suspiciously neat about scanning a place in 3D a few minutes before dismantling it in ordinary space.

With the visa finally in hand, the move to Italy entered its least abstract phase: shelves, tools, books, and departures. The festival, rivers, exhibitions, and films kept the month from turning into pure logistics. July felt less like leaving Tokyo than trying to finish the conversation properly before going.
