date: 2026-06-30

- Got married in Rome. The week around it was full of friends, family, aperitivi, airport runs, and the particular relief of seeing everyone happily mixed together.
- Kept turning the Rome studio into a real working space: installed the light fixtures, added a proper espresso machine, built out more storage, and started turning the former “darkroom” into a storage and 3D-printing room.
- Published [Triage](/obsidian/triage/), an [Obsidian](https://obsidian.md/) plugin for resurfacing unprocessed notes in an Anki-style queue before they disappear into the vault.
- Announced [*Scent of Time*](/flaneur/014/), my photobook project from the [FotoFilmic Book Program](https://fotofilmic.com/fotofilmic-book-program/), and shared details for its first public showing in the [FBP 2025-26 Photobook Exhibitions](https://fotofilmic.com/fbp-exhibition-2025-26/) on Bowen Island.
- Finished another sequencing pass for *Scent of Time* and started preparing the next physical version for printing.
- Published three notes around interfaces and spatial computing: [Attention Platforms Cannot Invent New Interfaces](/notes/attention-platforms-cannot-invent-new-interfaces/), [Hands Are Not Cursors](/notes/hands-are-not-cursors/), and [Headsets and Glasses Are a Stepping Stone at Best](/notes/headsets-and-glasses-are-a-stepping-stone-at-best/).
- Returned to Tokyo after the wedding stretch and started settling back into the Kyojima studio rhythm.
- Picked up a new compact monochrome camera and started thinking again about the next [Out of Memory](https://toto.photo/out-of-memory) edit.
- Spent a weekend in Abruzzo, attended another wedding, and had a long seafood lunch on a [trabocco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trabocco), which is a deeply civilized way to turn a fishing platform into an eating platform.
- Updated parts of [toto.photo](https://toto.photo/) while continuing to shape the photo side of the work around *Scent of Time* and *Out of Memory*.

June was a threshold month: partly ceremony, partly travel, partly cleanup after ceremony and travel. The wedding made the month feel unusually public and social, while the work that followed pulled me back toward quieter things: sequencing photographs, writing notes, setting up rooms, and making the tools around the work a little sharper.

What I like about the month in retrospect is that none of those threads felt separate. The studio, the book, the website, the plugins, even the slow return to Tokyo all felt like different ways of making the same life slightly more usable.
